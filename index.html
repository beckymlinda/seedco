Common one-stroke penalties

  • Relief from a penalty area (Rule 17), lost ball or out of bounds (18), unplayable ball (19)
  • Player accidentally moves their own ball at rest, outside the exceptions (9.4)
  • Lifting a ball without marking it when the Rules say mark it (14.1)
  • Removing a loose impediment causes the ball to move, off the green (15.1)
  • Ball overhanging the hole drops in after the 10-second wait (13.3)

Common general penalties (2 strokes / loss of hole)

  • Playing a wrong ball (6.3c) or from a wrong place (14.7)
  • Improving the lie, stance, swing area or line of play (8.1)
  • Giving or asking for advice (10.2), anchoring the club (10.1b)
  • Testing the sand in a bunker or touching it with the club right behind the ball (12.2)
  • In stroke play: a putt from the green hits another ball at rest on the green (11.1a)

Multiple breaches

One act, or a series of related acts, normally gets only one penalty (the higher one if they differ). See Rule 1.3c(4).

Club-length = the longest club in the bag other than the putter. The ball is dropped from knee height, must land in and stay in the relief area, and must not be nearer the hole than the reference point.

Back-on-line relief

Keep the reference point (where the ball lies, or for a penalty area where it last crossed the edge) between the hole and the spot you pick on that line. No limit how far back. Drop on the line; the ball must stay within 1 club-length of where it first touches the ground, not nearer the hole than the spot on the line.

Dropping procedure (14.3)

  1. Drop straight down from knee height; the ball must not hit the player or equipment before landing.
  2. It must land in the relief area and come to rest in it.
  3. If not, drop a second time.
  4. If it fails again, place it where it first touched the ground on the second drop.

A substitute ball may be used whenever taking relief.

These are Model Local Rules. They apply only if your Committee announces them for the competition.

  • E-3 Preferred lies / lift, clean and place: in wet or poor conditions, the ball may be lifted, cleaned and placed within a set distance (often one club-length or a scorecard length) in the area the Committee specifies.
  • E-5 Alternative to stroke and distance: for a lost or out-of-bounds ball, the player may drop near where it was lost or went out, including on the edge of the fairway, for 2 penalty strokes. Not meant for elite competitions.
  • B-2 Extra option for red penalty areas: drop on the opposite side, the same distance from the hole, for 1 stroke.
  • F-2 Embedded ball: can limit relief to closely mown areas, or extend it.
  • G-5 Distance-measuring devices: DMDs are allowed by default; this Local Rule bans them.
  • Dropping zones (E-1): extra relief option, often at penalty areas.

  • General area: the whole course except the teeing area of the hole, bunkers, penalty areas and the putting green of the hole.
  • Abnormal course condition: animal hole, ground under repair, immovable obstruction, or temporary water.
  • Loose impediment: unattached natural object, such as a stone, loose grass, leaf, twig, insect, dung or worm cast.
  • Movable obstruction: artificial object that can be moved easily without damage, such as a rake, bottle or bag.
  • Known or virtually certain: at least 95% sure, based on all the evidence. The test for whether a ball went into a penalty area or was moved by the player.
  • Nearest point of complete relief: the closest spot, not nearer the hole and in the required area, where the condition no longer interferes with lie, stance or swing.
  • Outside influence: any person or thing (other than the player, caddie, partner, opponents and their equipment) that may affect the ball, such as another player, a spectator or an animal. Wind and water are natural forces, not outside influences.
  • Ball in play: after a stroke from the teeing area until holed, lifted, lost, out of bounds or replaced by another ball.
  • Serious breach: playing from a wrong place that gives a significant advantage. Must be corrected or the player is disqualified.

The 25 Rules

Key points

  • Play the course as you find it and the ball as it lies.
  • Players are expected to act with integrity, show consideration and care for the course. The Committee may set a Code of Conduct with its own penalties.
  • Serious misconduct can lead to disqualification.
  • Players are responsible for applying the Rules to themselves and calling their own penalties. Deliberately ignoring a known breach can lead to disqualification.
  • If a player makes an honest estimate of a spot or line, it is accepted even if later shown to be wrong.
  • Players in match play may not agree to ignore a Rule or penalty they know applies; both are disqualified.

Penalty

Penalties are one stroke, the general penalty (2 strokes / loss of hole) or disqualification. One act or related acts normally get one penalty only.
Full Rule 1 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • The Committee sets the boundaries; anything beyond is out of bounds, usually marked by white stakes or lines.
  • A ball is out of bounds only when all of it is outside the boundary edge.
  • The five areas: general area, teeing area (of the hole being played), bunkers, penalty areas, and the putting green (of the hole being played).
  • Areas can also be marked as no play zones, where play is not allowed and relief must be taken.
  • Boundary objects (walls, fences, stakes marking OB) are not obstructions: no free relief.
Full Rule 2 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Match play: player or side wins a hole with fewer strokes; the match ends when one side leads by more holes than are left.
  • In match play, strokes, holes or the match may be conceded at any time. A concession cannot be withdrawn.
  • In match play, tell the opponent about any penalty as soon as possible, and give the correct number of strokes if asked. Giving a wrong number and not correcting it before the opponent plays again costs the hole.
  • Stroke play: the player with the lowest total score wins. The ball must be holed on every hole (except in Stableford, Maximum Score and Par/Bogey).
  • Scorecard: the marker certifies the hole scores, the player checks, certifies and returns it promptly.
  • Hole score higher than actual: the higher score stands. Lower than actual: disqualification.
  • Exception: if a lower score is because the player didn't know about a penalty, no disqualification; add the penalty plus 2 extra strokes on that hole.
  • In handicap competitions the Committee is responsible for applying handicaps; players are responsible for the correct gross score on each hole.

Penalty

Wrong (lower) hole score returned: disqualification, subject to the exception above.
Full Rule 3 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Only conforming clubs and balls may be used. A club whose playing characteristics were deliberately changed during the round must not be used.
  • A club damaged during the round (unless through abuse) may be used, repaired or replaced.
  • Maximum 14 clubs. A player who starts with fewer may add clubs up to 14, without delaying play or borrowing from someone on the course.
  • If an excess club is discovered, declare it out of play immediately (for example, turn it upside down in the bag).
  • Distance-measuring devices are allowed for distance unless a Local Rule bans them. Devices that measure slope or give club or swing advice must not be used during the round.
  • No artificial devices for unusual help (e.g. alignment aids placed down, devices measuring wind).

Penalty

Too many clubs: stroke play 2 strokes per hole where breached, maximum 4. Match play: match adjusted by one hole per hole breached, maximum two. Using a non-conforming club: disqualification.
Full Rule 4 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Arrive and start at the starting time. Up to 5 minutes late (and ready to play) or up to 5 minutes early: general penalty applied to the first hole. More than 5 minutes: disqualification.
  • Stroke play: no practice on the competition course before the round on the day. Match play: allowed unless the Terms say otherwise.
  • During the round, no practice strokes, except chipping or putting on or near the green just completed, practice greens, or the next teeing area (not from a bunker).
  • Play without undue delay. The recommended time for a stroke is no more than 40 seconds once it is the player's turn.
  • Stop immediately when the Committee suspends play for danger (e.g. lightning). For a normal suspension, a player may finish the hole.
  • Players may stop on their own if they reasonably believe there is lightning danger, and must report to the Committee.
  • When resuming, play from where play stopped; mark and lift a ball before stopping if needed.

Penalty

Late within 5 minutes: 2 strokes / loss of first hole. Practice before round in stroke play: general penalty, then disqualification for a second breach. Undue delay: 1 stroke, then general penalty, then disqualification.
Full Rule 5 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Start each hole from inside the teeing area: up to two club-lengths deep behind the front of the tee markers.
  • The ball is in the teeing area if any part of it touches or is above the teeing area. The player may stand outside it.
  • A ball that falls off the tee (or is knocked off) before a stroke is made may be re-teed without penalty.
  • Play out the hole with the ball played from the tee, unless the Rules allow a substitute.
  • Playing a wrong ball: penalty, the strokes with the wrong ball don't count, and the player must then play the right ball (or take relief).
  • Match play: the player farther from the hole plays first; playing out of turn lets the opponent cancel the stroke. Stroke play: playing out of turn is not penalised; "ready golf" is encouraged.
  • Stroke play: hole out on every hole before teeing off on the next.

Penalty

Playing from outside the teeing area: stroke play 2 strokes and play again from inside (failure to correct before the next tee: disqualification); match play opponent may cancel the stroke. Wrong ball: general penalty; in stroke play correct before teeing off on the next hole, or disqualification.
Full Rule 6 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • A player may search fairly for the ball, including moving sand or grass, but must not improve conditions more than reasonable to find or identify it.
  • The search time is 3 minutes. A ball not found in 3 minutes is lost.
  • To identify a ball, the player may lift it after marking the spot, and clean it only as much as needed to identify it.
  • If a ball is accidentally moved by anyone during the search or identification, there is no penalty: replace it on its original spot (estimate if not known).
  • If sand was moved to find a ball in a bunker, re-create the original lie, leaving a small part of the ball visible.

Penalty

Lifting to identify without marking, or cleaning more than needed: one stroke.
Full Rule 7 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Do not improve the conditions affecting the stroke: lie, area of intended stance, area of intended swing, line of play, or the relief area where a ball will be dropped.
  • Prohibited examples: moving, bending or breaking growing things or fixed objects (including boundary stakes); pressing a club down behind the ball; removing dew or frost; building a stance.
  • Allowed: taking a stance fairly, making a stroke or backswing, grounding the club lightly, repairing the teeing area or putting green as the Rules allow.
  • If someone else worsens the conditions after the ball came to rest (e.g. stands on the line, a divot lands on the ball), the player may restore them.
  • Before the stroke, some improvements can be undone to avoid a penalty, but not if growing things were bent or broken or objects were moved into place.

Penalty

General penalty.
Full Rule 8 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Play the ball where it lies, except where the Rules allow relief.
  • A ball has "moved" only if it leaves its spot and comes to rest somewhere else; wobbling and returning does not count.
  • It must be known or virtually certain that the player caused it to move; otherwise assume natural forces.
  • Natural forces (wind, gravity) move the ball: no penalty, play it from its new spot. Exception: on the putting green, after it was lifted and replaced, replace it.
  • Player moves own ball: one stroke and replace it. No penalty if it happens during search, accidentally on the putting green, or accidentally while applying a Rule (e.g. marking, measuring).
  • Opponent in match play or an outside influence (another player, animal) moves it: no penalty, replace it.
  • A ball moved after the stroke began and played anyway: the stroke counts, no replacing.

Penalty

Player lifts or moves own ball when not allowed: one stroke, and replace it. Not replacing when required and playing it: general penalty.
Full Rule 9 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Strike the ball fairly with the head of the club; no pushing, scraping or scooping.
  • No anchoring the club against the body, directly or by using a forearm as an anchor point.
  • Advice (anything that could influence club choice, how to play, or the stroke) must not be given to or asked from anyone except a partner, caddies, or a team captain/coach if the Terms allow.
  • Public information (distances, Rules, hole locations) is not advice.
  • Nobody may place an object to help with alignment; the caddie must not stand on or near the line behind the ball while the player makes the stroke.
  • A caddie must not deliberately stand behind the player on the line of play as the player starts taking their stance for the stroke.
  • One caddie at a time. The player is responsible for the caddie's breaches.

Penalty

General penalty (each breach of advice or caddie positioning; for having more than one caddie, for each hole it happened).
Full Rule 10 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • If a ball in motion accidentally hits any person or outside influence, there is no penalty; play the ball as it lies.
  • Exception (stroke play): if a putt from the green hits another ball at rest on the green, the player gets the general penalty.
  • A ball played from the putting green that accidentally hits a person, animal or movable obstruction (other than limited exceptions such as the flagstick, a ball-marker or another ball) must be replayed without penalty.
  • If someone deliberately deflects or stops a ball in motion, special rules apply; usually the stroke is replayed or the ball placed where it would have come to rest.
  • Nobody may deliberately move objects, change conditions or lift balls to affect where a moving ball might come to rest (a flagstick or ball can be lifted under specific Rules).

Penalty

Putt from the green hits another ball at rest on the green, in stroke play: general penalty. Deliberately affecting a ball in motion: general penalty.
Full Rule 11 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • A ball is in a bunker when any part of it touches the sand inside the bunker edge, or it lies inside the edge on soil or a loose impediment.
  • Loose impediments and movable obstructions may be removed. If the ball moves when a loose impediment is removed, one stroke and replace it.
  • Do not deliberately touch the sand to test it, touch the sand with the club right in front of or behind the ball, or in a practice swing or the backswing.
  • Allowed: digging feet in for a stance, smoothing sand to care for the course, leaning on a club for balance, placing clubs in the bunker, hitting the sand in frustration.
  • After the ball is out of the bunker, the player may smooth the sand freely.
  • Unplayable ball in bunker: see Rule 19.3. Abnormal course conditions in bunker: see Rule 16.1c.

Penalty

Touching sand improperly: general penalty.
Full Rule 12 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • A ball on the putting green may be marked, lifted and cleaned at any time; it must be replaced on its original spot.
  • Repair almost any damage on the green: ball marks, shoe damage, old hole plugs, scratches, animal damage, embedded objects. Not natural surface imperfections, aeration holes or natural wear of the hole.
  • No penalty if the player, opponent or caddie accidentally moves the ball or ball-marker on the green: replace it.
  • A ball replaced on the green that then moves by natural forces must be replaced.
  • Do not deliberately test the green by rubbing the surface or rolling a ball.
  • The flagstick may stay in, be removed or be attended. No penalty if a ball played hits the flagstick left in the hole.
  • A ball resting against the flagstick is holed if any part of it is below the surface of the green.
  • A ball overhanging the hole: allow reasonable time to reach the hole plus 10 seconds. If it falls in later, it is holed with one penalty stroke.

Penalty

Testing the green or improper repair: general penalty. Overhanging ball falls in after the waiting time: one stroke.
Full Rule 13 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Mark the spot before lifting (when replacing is required) with a ball-marker placed right behind or beside the ball, or by holding a club next to it.
  • Only the player or someone they authorise may lift the ball; only the player or the person who lifted it may replace it.
  • Replace on the original spot. If the spot is unknown, estimate it.
  • Dropping: from knee height, straight down, landing in the relief area and staying in it. Drop again if it doesn't; if the second drop fails, place where it first touched the ground.
  • A ball dropped or placed in the wrong way can be lifted and corrected without penalty before the stroke.
  • Playing from a wrong place: general penalty. If it is a serious breach (a significant advantage), it must be corrected by playing from the right place before teeing off on the next hole, or the player is disqualified.
  • In stroke play, if unsure whether a serious breach occurred, the player may also play a second ball from the right place and report to the Committee.

Penalty

Lifting without marking: one stroke. Playing a ball dropped or placed incorrectly: general penalty (or one stroke in some cases). Wrong place: general penalty; uncorrected serious breach: disqualification.
Full Rule 14 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Loose impediments may be removed without penalty anywhere on or off the course, in any way.
  • If removing a loose impediment causes the ball to move, one penalty stroke and replace it (no penalty on the putting green or teeing area).
  • Movable obstructions may be removed anywhere without penalty. If the ball moves as a result, no penalty: replace it.
  • If the ball lies in or on a movable obstruction (except on the green), lift it, remove the obstruction, and drop within one club-length of the spot right under the ball. On the green, place it.
  • A ball that might help or interfere with another player's play may be marked and lifted on request; players must not agree to leave a ball in place to help someone.

Penalty

Ball moves when removing a loose impediment (off green and teeing area): one stroke and replace.
Full Rule 15 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Abnormal course conditions are animal holes, ground under repair, immovable obstructions (e.g. cart paths, sprinkler heads) and temporary water.
  • Interference means the condition touches the ball, or is in the area of intended stance or swing. On the green, also the line of play.
  • No free relief if the ball is in a penalty area, or if it is clearly unreasonable to play because of something else (e.g. ball in a bush), or the stance is clearly unreasonable.
  • General area: find the nearest point of complete relief, then drop within one club-length of it, not nearer the hole, in the general area.
  • Bunker: free relief in the bunker, or back-on-line outside the bunker for one stroke.
  • Putting green: place the ball at the nearest point of complete relief, which may be off the green.
  • Dangerous animal condition (snakes, bees, alligators etc.): relief allowed anywhere, including in a penalty area.
  • Embedded ball (in its own pitch mark in the general area): free relief within one club-length of the spot right behind the ball. Not available if embedded in sand in an area not cut to fairway height or less. A Local Rule can change this.
  • The ball must be known or virtually certain to be in the condition to get relief if it is not found; then use the estimated spot.

Penalty

No penalty for correctly taking relief; dropping or playing incorrectly can lead to the general penalty.
Full Rule 16 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Penalty areas are marked red or yellow. A ball is in a penalty area when any part of it touches the area.
  • The player may play the ball as it lies, with the same freedoms as in the general area (can ground the club, remove loose impediments, touch water) without penalty.
  • It must be known or virtually certain that the ball is in the penalty area to use these options; otherwise treat it as lost (Rule 18).
  • Yellow, one penalty stroke: (a) stroke and distance, or (b) back-on-line, keeping the point where the ball last crossed the edge between the hole and the drop spot.
  • Red, one penalty stroke: the yellow options, or (c) lateral relief, dropping within two club-lengths of where the ball last crossed the edge, not nearer the hole.
  • No free relief from abnormal course conditions, no embedded ball relief and no unplayable ball relief inside a penalty area. Dangerous animal relief is still allowed.
  • A ball played from a penalty area that comes to rest in the same or another penalty area, or goes out of bounds or is lost, has extra options under Rule 17.2.

Penalty

One penalty stroke for any relief option. Playing a ball that was not known or virtually certain to be in the penalty area as if it were: wrong place.
Full Rule 17 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Stroke and distance is always available: one penalty stroke, play again from where the previous stroke was made.
  • A ball is lost if it is not found within 3 minutes of starting to search. Out of bounds or lost: one penalty stroke and play again from the previous spot.
  • If the ball might be lost outside a penalty area or out of bounds, the player may play a provisional ball to save time.
  • The player must announce it as a "provisional" before playing it, and before going forward to search for the original.
  • The player may keep playing the provisional until reaching the area where the original is likely to be.
  • If the original is found on the course within 3 minutes, the provisional must be abandoned and the original played.
  • If the original is lost or out of bounds, the provisional becomes the ball in play with one penalty stroke.
  • A provisional ball is not allowed if the only place the original could be is a penalty area.
  • Local Rule E-5 (if adopted) gives a 2-stroke alternative option near where the ball was lost or went out of bounds.

Penalty

One penalty stroke. Not announcing a provisional: that ball becomes the ball in play under stroke and distance.
Full Rule 18 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • The player alone decides when a ball is unplayable, anywhere on the course except in a penalty area.
  • One penalty stroke, three options: (a) stroke and distance; (b) back-on-line, keeping the ball's spot between the hole and the drop spot; (c) lateral relief within two club-lengths of the ball, not nearer the hole.
  • For options (b) and (c) the ball must be identified only if it is found; for (a) the ball does not need to be found.
  • In a bunker: the same three options, but (b) and (c) must be in the bunker. Or for 2 penalty strokes in total, back-on-line outside the bunker.
  • A ball stuck in a tree can be treated as unplayable if it is identified; lateral relief is measured from the spot on the ground right below it.

Penalty

One penalty stroke (two strokes total for back-on-line relief outside a bunker).
Full Rule 19 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Match play: if there is a dispute, a player may ask for a ruling. The request must be made in time: before either player starts the next hole (or on the final hole, before the result is final).
  • Stroke play: if unsure what to do, the player may play two balls, announce which one should count if the Rules allow, and finish the hole with both.
  • The player must report the facts to the Committee before returning the scorecard, even if both scores are the same.
  • A referee's ruling is final. Players must follow it. The Committee can correct a wrong ruling as long as the competition is not closed.
  • Penalties can be applied after the competition ends only in limited cases (e.g. a player knowingly returned a wrong score).

Penalty

Not reporting two-ball play to the Committee before returning the scorecard: disqualification.
Full Rule 20 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Stableford: points per hole by comparing the score to a fixed target (usually net par). Double bogey or worse = 0 points; the player may pick up once they cannot score points.
  • Maximum Score: hole scores are capped at a maximum set by the Committee (e.g. net double bogey or a fixed number). Picking up at the cap is allowed.
  • Par/Bogey: like match play against a fixed score; a hole is won, lost or halved compared with the target.
  • Three-Ball match play: each player plays two separate matches at the same time against the other two.
  • Stableford/Maximum Score scorecard: a hole score that is too low only gets that hole penalised (e.g. 0 points, or the maximum score) if it affects the result, not disqualification.
  • In Stableford, a general penalty is added to the hole score; for some breaches (e.g. too many clubs, undue delay) points are deducted from the total.

Penalty

Rules mainly change how penalties and scores are applied; see the Rule for details.
Full Rule 21 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Two partners play one ball, taking turns to make strokes.
  • One partner tees off on odd-numbered holes, the other on even-numbered holes.
  • Penalty strokes do not change the order of play.
  • A provisional ball or stroke-and-distance replay is played by the partner whose turn it is next.
  • Either partner may take actions for the side, such as marking, lifting or dropping.
  • A stroke made by the wrong partner: general penalty, and the side must correct the order.

Penalty

Playing in the wrong order: general penalty; in stroke play correct before teeing off on the next hole, or the side is disqualified.
Full Rule 22 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Two partners each play their own ball; the lower score on each hole is the side's score.
  • A side may be represented by one partner for all or part of the round; the absent partner may join between holes.
  • Partners may play in any order the side chooses.
  • Stroke play scorecard: each partner's gross scores must be clearly identified so the Committee knows which score counts.
  • A breach by one partner usually affects only that partner's score, but sometimes the partner is also penalised (e.g. if the breach helped them).

Penalty

Depends on the breach; often only the partner who breached is penalised for that hole.
Full Rule 23 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • In a team competition, team scores are made up from individual or partner results as set in the Terms of the Competition.
  • The Committee may allow each team to name a captain and/or an advice giver who may give advice to team members.
  • The Terms of the Competition decide how scores count and what penalties apply to the team.
Full Rule 24 on randa.org ↗

Key points

  • Sets out changes to the Rules for players with specific disabilities so they can play fairly with others.
  • Players who are blind: may have help with alignment and may ground the club in a bunker before the stroke.
  • Players who are amputees: may use prosthetic devices and approved assistive aids.
  • Players who use assistive mobility devices (e.g. wheelchairs): relief areas and procedures are adjusted.
  • Players with intellectual impairment: may have a supervisor or assistant.
  • These modifications apply automatically for eligible players; the Committee can decide on eligibility.
